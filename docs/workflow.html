SalesEngine — what it does, end to end

One prospect's journey through the system. Who does each part, and what it leaves behind.

you, by hand the app, on its own LinkedIn email

Find themyouSales Navigator

Sales Navigator search → a lead list

You do the searching, because this is the part that needs judgement about who is actually worth talking to. Sales Navigator has no export, so the list moves into a spreadsheet by hand: profile URL, name, title, company — plus industry and headcount, which are on the same screen and are what the drafts later read.

Leaves: a CSV
2

Importapp

LinkedIn queue → Import a lead list

Keyed on profile URL, so re-importing the same list updates rather than duplicates. People at one company are grouped into one Account. Each person is scored on seniority, function and company fit.

Leaves: Contacts + Accounts, scored, in your database
3

Open the relationshipyou sendLinkedIn

LinkedIn queue → Build target list → work the cards

The app picks the highest-scoring people, drafts a connection note from facts on the record, and checks it against LinkedIn's length limit. You press Copy & open profile, paste, send — in your own browser, in your own session. The app never touches LinkedIn, which is why your account is not at risk. You click I sent it and that goes on the timeline as your word, not an observation.

Capped at 20 connection requests a day. Not evasion — that's roughly where acceptance rates fall and people start reporting you.

Leaves: an activity timeline, and eventually a connection
4

Follow up by emailapp sendsMicrosoft 365

Sequences → enrol the contact

This is the part that actually runs unattended. A sequence is a series of emails with waits between them. The worker sends them inside your business hours, respects a daily per-mailbox cap, and appends an unsubscribe footer. Needs an email address — see the note below.

Leaves: sent mail, opens and clicks, an outbox record per send
5

Catch the replyappGraph API

Inbox

The worker polls your mailbox through Microsoft Graph, matches each reply to a contact, and classifies it: interested, not interested, out of office, bounce, unsubscribe. An interested reply stops the sequence immediately — the single most important thing here, because nothing damages a relationship faster than a fifth automated email after someone has already answered.

Out-of-office holds the sequence until they're back. A bounce marks the address dead. An opt-out is honoured permanently.

Leaves: a reply in your Inbox, sequence stopped, contact re-scored
★

Campaigns tie 3–5 togetherapp decidesyou send

Sequences → build a campaign → enrol contacts

Everything above is one prospect. A campaign runs that shape over a whole list and decides, per person, what happens next:

1  connect  ← you send
2  wait 3 days
3  if they accepted → message  ← you send
4  wait 4 days
5  if still no reply → email  ← app sends

The LinkedIn steps stop and wait for you. The campaign does not advance until you actually work the card, so it can never claim to have sent something you didn’t. A card left untouched for two weeks skips that step rather than parking forever and looking busy.

The “if they accepted” branch works because acceptance now arrives on its own — read from LinkedIn’s own notification email in your inbox, not from LinkedIn itself.

Leaves: a funnel — enrolled → invited → accepted → replied, and where they drop
6

Work the dealyou

Deals → pipeline stages · Tasks · Reports

Once someone is talking to you, they become a Deal on a pipeline with stages you control. Tasks are your follow-ups. Reports tell you which step of which sequence actually produced the conversations — which is how you decide what to change.

Leaves: a pipeline you can forecast from

Where does Apollo fit?

Nowhere, in the flow above — and that's deliberate. Sales Navigator already gives you name, title, company, industry and headcount. Apollo would only add one thing you can't get yourself: the work email address.

That matters, because stage 4 cannot run without one. With Sales Navigator alone you have a LinkedIn-only motion — stages 1–3 and 6, no email sequencing.

If you decide you want email, the cheap route is Apollo's $49 UI plan and its CSV export, not the API — API access starts around $79–119/seat. Export from Apollo, import here, done. Same importer, no key, no code. Check the export credit limit on Basic before you commit.

not yet

Why is there a CRM connector?

For your own use, there's nothing in it. SalesEngine is your CRM — Contacts, Accounts, Deals, pipeline, activity history all live here.

The Salesforce connector exists for the version of this you sell to someone else. A company that already runs on Salesforce won't move off it; they want this as the outbound engine bolted onto the system of record they already have. The sync handles the hard parts of that — echo suppression, conflict policy, watermarks.

Leave it disconnected. Nothing degrades.
